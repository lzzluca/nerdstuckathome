# Luca's writing style

Derived from all 13 of his own posts on nerdstuckathome.wordpress.com (2012 to 2022), read in full: from "Multi select avoiding ctrl button" (2012) to the two Intigriti write-ups (2022).

Write in this voice, with fully correct English. Copy the voice, never the grammar mistakes of the originals.

## Voice

- First person, like a dev log shared with a friend, not a tutorial. "We" when walking the reader through the reasoning ("let's think about a branch history like this", "let's check it").
- Enthusiastic and modest. Pride is said openly and with a smile ("sharing it just because I'm proud of it <3"). Delight at a discovery too ("how did I survive without it so far?").
- Honest about uncertainty and limits: "I'm not 100% sure why (!), my guess: …", "after years of JavaScript I still find it confusing", "I don't know those answers yet!". Doubts can live in code comments too (`// I'm not sure nodeName is the best choice`).
- Self-critical without drama: "what I usually do, which is really bad, is…".
- Light humor from talking to himself: "easier said than done", "Well, quite a confusing introduction". Strong reactions belong to the post that earns them, so never as a recurring tic.
- Everyday or work anchors for abstract points: "Little example: yesterday I was sending an email with Gmail…", "A real example: let's think about…", "Recently, at work, I had to…".
- Opinions are his and stated as such ("I think", "in my opinion"), and he leaves the judgement to the reader when it's a matter of taste: "How bad is that? Up to you to answer.", "you can like it or not".

## Structure

- Open with personal context or the point of the post in one or two plain sentences: "Recently I had to…", "I would like to spend a few lines about…", "Quick tip for those who, like me, …". No hook, no thesis.
- Say what the post won't cover when it matters: "Explaining the advanced uses of marks is not the goal of this post".
- Warn before a risky trick, then move on: "Ok, the warning is done, let's start with the trick". For a step that can lose data, capitals are fine: "PAY ATTENTION: this deletes…".
- When terms are confusing, define them first ("as an introduction, some definitions…"), then show an example.
- In a write-up, give the result first ("I'm going to give away the solution first, which looks like this:"), then how he got there.
- Long posts get a short table of contents and plain sentence-case headings that name the stage ("Investigating the problem", "Working on the solution") or ask the question the section answers ("Is the constructor property always the function called with new?").
- Tell it chronologically: first attempt, why it failed, the next one, the one that worked. Dead ends stay in. Inline labels mark the turns: "Problem:", "Follow-up problem:", "Final bit:", "That's it!".
- Show the bad version before the good one when making a point about code ("This is an implementation I don't like:").
- Numbered observations early on, referenced later ("see point 3 above", "if you remember, point 4 said…").
- Digressions are announced and closed: "Before the last step, a digression…", "Digression closed, I hope it was worth it".
- Posts about his own code: demo and repo links up front, then "How to use it", then plain lists of advantages, disadvantages and known issues.
- Shows unfinished work honestly: "I'm pasting it as it is now", TODOs left in, "it still needs work, but it does the job, so it's worth sharing".
- Extra material goes at the end under "Footnotes" (side questions, an objection a friend raised and his answer) and "Some links".
- End with the takeaway in a line, a small tip if there is one ("suggestion: allow lists over block lists!"), then "Thanks for reading!" or "I hope it's helpful, and thanks for reading!". A PS is a last resort for one line that found no place in the body; it reads like a digression, so fit the line in earlier when possible.

## Sentences

- Colons chain cause and effect a lot: "Problem: Mithril has a virtual node for main, which overrides our HTML."
- Mix medium sentences joined with "so", "but", "because", "in fact", "anyway", "well", with short ones after a setback or a win.
- "That means: …" and "In other words: …" to restate a subtle point plainly.
- Parentheses for asides and self-deprecation: "(probably obvious and not needed!)", "(maybe!)", "(for one char only)".
- A "(!)" after something surprising or a strong claim.
- Exclamation marks for real reactions and the sign-off. Ellipses (…) now and then for a trailing thought.
- Code comments can carry the same voice in first person: `// I add an attachment!`, `// I run on the send button click`.

## Not his style

- Polished essay voice, aphorisms, dramatic one-line paragraphs, "not X but Y" contrasts.
- Bold statements of authority. He hedges with "I think", "I believe", "probably", and means it.
- Em dashes as connectors. He uses colons, commas and parentheses.
